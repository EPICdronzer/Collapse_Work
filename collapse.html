Situational Awareness LP: The Rise, Near-Collapse, and Restructuring of an AI-Concentrated Hedge Fund

01 — Executive Summary

A $45B thesis-driven fund, a four-times-levered book, and a 24-hour forced sale

Situational Awareness LP, an AI-focused hedge fund founded in July 2024 by 24-year-old former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, grew in roughly two years into one of the most closely watched vehicles in the AI trade — reportedly returning over 1,000% since inception and peaking near $45 billion in AUM at the start of July 2026, on the back of a reported 439% year-to-date return through June.

In late July 2026, a sharp reversal in AI-infrastructure equities, combined with losses on the fund's short software positions, triggered a self-reinforcing deleveraging event. Reported leverage of up to 4x turned a sector correction into a liquidity crisis. Margin calls from the fund's three prime brokers forced a sale of essentially its entire public equity portfolio — roughly two-thirds of holdings — to Citadel at a distressed price over about 24 hours.

The fund was not dissolved. Its unmargined private holdings — dominated by a reported $5B stake in Anthropic, plus smaller positions in Fluidstack and MatX — carried no collateral risk and survived intact. Days later, the fund reportedly closed a new $400M private investment. This reads less as a fund's extinction than a near-fatal deleveraging of its public book, driven by structural risk-management failures rather than a wholesale invalidation of its thesis.

02 — Background of the Founder

Leopold Aschenbrenner

  • Enrolled at Columbia University at 15, graduated valedictorian at 19.
  • Worked in EA-aligned grant-making at Sam Bankman-Fried's FTX Future Fund — an association later scrutinized given FTX's collapse, though Aschenbrenner was not implicated in its wrongdoing.
  • Joined OpenAI's Superalignment team in 2023, researching long-horizon AI safety.
  • Was dismissed roughly a year later over a disputed characterization of an internal-information disclosure.
  • In mid-2024 published "Situational Awareness," a 165-page essay on scaling laws and the compute/power build-out they'd require — the essay became the fund's namesake and intellectual foundation.

The fund launched in July 2024, seeded by Stripe's Patrick and John Collison, former GitHub CEO Nat Friedman, and investor Daniel Gross. Aschenbrenner had no prior professional experience managing capital or trading public markets before founding it.

03 — Strategy & Portfolio Construction

Concentrated, correlated, and highly levered

Long book

AI-infrastructure "picks and shovels": memory and semiconductor names (SK Hynix, Micron, Sandisk), AI cloud/compute providers (CoreWeave, Nebius, SharonAI, IREN), and power infrastructure (Bloom Energy).

Short book

Bearish bets on legacy software names viewed as vulnerable to AI disruption, reportedly including Adobe.

Private holdings

Roughly a third of the portfolio, dominated by a stake in Anthropic bought at a ~$60B valuation in February 2025 — worth an estimated $5B by mid-2026 after Anthropic's Series H at a reported $965B valuation — plus smaller stakes in Fluidstack and MatX.

Leverage

Up to roughly 4x on the public book. Because the long and short legs were both exposed to the same underlying AI-sentiment factor rather than genuinely hedged, the leverage compounded a correlated bet — a broad reversal in AI sentiment could, and did, hit both sides of the book at once.

Mechanics of the collapse

  • A sector-wide price reversal reduced the mark-to-market value of the fund's collateral.
  • Prime-broker maintenance requirements scale non-linearly as collateral value falls, rapidly increasing cash demands.
  • Losses hit the long and short books simultaneously — a correlated, not offsetting, structure — limiting the fund's capacity to raise cash internally.
  • Forced selling signaled distress to the market; other participants reportedly traded against the position, echoing dynamics from Archegos Capital Management's 2021 collapse.
  • With no fresh capital by the brokers' deadline, the fund sold its public book in bulk; Citadel absorbed the bulk of it at a discount.
  • Because private holdings carried no margin, they were insulated from repricing and preserved — letting the firm survive as a smaller, private-focused vehicle.
05 — Risk Management Failures & Key Takeaways

Core failures

  • Inexperience in professional risk management — Wall Street risk professionals reportedly saw a blow-up of this kind as "a matter of not if, but when" once the fund's leverage became known.
  • Extreme leverage relative to strategy concentration — 4x is aggressive even for diversified strategies; applied to a single-theme book it left little margin for a sector-wide reversal.
  • False diversification — the long/short structure looked hedged but both legs shared the same AI-sentiment exposure, so losses compounded rather than offset.
  • Liquidity mismatch under stress — public disclosure of investor-letter losses appears to have invited further selling pressure, compounding the forced liquidation.

Conviction vs. competence

Aschenbrenner's case is often cited to illustrate a broader pattern: deep domain expertise and analytical conviction — here, about AI capability trajectories — don't necessarily come with the distinct skills of public-markets risk management, position sizing, and leverage discipline. Some commentators frame this as Dunning-Kruger-adjacent; others note the fund's long-term AI-infrastructure thesis may still prove directionally correct, and CNBC's Jim Cramer characterized the forced selling as a possible "clearing event" for the broader AI trade.

"A matter of not if, but when" — an industry risk commentator, on the fund's leverage levels prior to the collapse.

Broader implications

  • AI investment thesis: not invalidated on its own — the untouched Anthropic stake continued to be viewed as an appreciating asset — but the episode shows even directionally correct theses can produce catastrophic short-term outcomes when over-levered.
  • Hedge fund industry: joins a lineage of leverage-driven failures — LTCM, Amaranth, Archegos — reinforcing that concentrated, highly leveraged single-theme strategies stay structurally fragile.
  • Prime brokerage risk controls: three major brokers extending ~4x leverage to a manager with no institutional risk track record raises questions about counterparty diligence during strong-performance periods.
  • Governance of "thought-leader" funds: highlights a growing category of vehicles built around a public intellectual's thesis and personal brand, and how such funds should be diligenced relative to traditional institutional managers.
06 — Postscript: Status as of Late Summer 2026

Deleveraging, not dissolution

Situational Awareness LP was not liquidated in the conventional sense. Following the forced sale of its public book, the firm retained its unmargined private holdings — most significantly its Anthropic stake — and has continued making new private investments, including a reported $400M commitment shortly after the crisis. The firm's public statements have denied reports that it sought to sell the Anthropic position. The episode is best characterized as a severe deleveraging of the fund's public portfolio, precipitated by classic risk-management failures, rather than the wholesale dissolution of the enterprise.
