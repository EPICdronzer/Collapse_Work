CLARITY ACT

Digital Asset Market Clarity Act — what it does, why it stalled in the Senate, and what it means for Indian crypto investors

Where the bill actually stands — updated Sept 16, 2026

The Senate rejected cloture 49–50 on Sept 15, falling 11 votes short of the 60 needed to even open floor debate — this was not the bill's final passage vote, but its failure means the Senate never gets that far this year. Several Democrats who had spent months negotiating the text (Gillibrand, Warner, Booker, Warnock, Gallego, Alsobrooks, Cortez Masto) voted no despite a revised, 630-page Republican draft released two days earlier that added ethics concessions. Sen. Cynthia Lummis, the bill's lead GOP sponsor, called it "now or never" beforehand and said afterward she believed the effort was over for this Congress. With roughly 22 legislative working days left before midterm campaigning consumes the calendar, market-structure legislation is now expected to wait until a new Congress — realistically 2027, and by some estimates as late as 2029–2030.

01What the Act does

The CLARITY Act is the first attempt at a full market-structure law for crypto in the U.S. — it doesn't ban or approve any coin, it decides who regulates what, ending years of the SEC and CFTC fighting over jurisdiction through lawsuits instead of rules.

CFTC

Digital commodities

Decentralized, mature networks — the bill's own examples are Bitcoin and Ether — move to CFTC oversight as commodities rather than securities.

SEC

Investment-contract assets

Tokens sold primarily to fund a business, with a controlling team behind them, stay under securities law and SEC disclosure rules.

Banking regulators

Permitted stablecoins

Payment stablecoins are supervised by banking regulators, building on the earlier GENIUS Act framework for reserves and redemption.

02Core provisions

✓
CFTC jurisdiction over spot markets

Gives the CFTC clear statutory authority over spot crypto trading, which it has largely lacked.

✓
"Maturity" test for decentralization

A legal test for when a blockchain network is decentralized enough to leave securities law behind.

✓
Customer asset segregation

Exchanges must keep customer funds separate from company funds — a direct response to the FTX collapse.

✓
Safe harbors for developers & DeFi

Protects software developers and non-custodial DeFi protocols from being treated as unlicensed brokers.

✓
Illicit-finance & ethics provisions

Anti-money-laundering rules, plus new ethics restrictions on officials' crypto holdings — the single biggest sticking point in the Senate.

03Timeline

JUL 2025

House passes CLARITY Act

Cleared the House 294–134 with bipartisan support and moved to the Senate.

MAY 2026

Senate Banking Committee markup

Committee advances a 309-page compromise text — including a DeFi framework and an insolvency safe harbor — to the full Senate.

AUG 2026

Senate misses its window

Chamber adjourns for August recess without a floor vote; Majority Leader Thune files cloture for September 15 to keep the bill alive.

SEP 15, 2026

Cloture vote fails, 49–50

The Senate falls 11 votes short of the 60 needed to open floor debate. Seven Democrats who had been negotiating the text for months vote no; Coinbase stock drops 8.65%, Bitcoin slides from near $78,000 to roughly $75,900–$76,000, and about $772 million in leveraged crypto positions are liquidated within 24 hours.

WHAT HAPPENS NOW

Bill effectively shelved for 2026

With cloture blocked, the Senate never opens formal debate, and only about 22 working days remain before midterm campaigning dominates the calendar. Lummis and other backers say a fresh attempt would likely need a new Congress — most estimates point to 2027 at the earliest, with some pushing the realistic window to 2029–2030. The SEC and CFTC continue to regulate crypto through existing rules and enforcement in the meantime.

04Market reaction — what actually happened

Confirmed outcome

Correction on failure

  • Bitcoin slid from near $78,000 to roughly $75,900–$76,000 (down ~3–4% on the day) as the vote failed
  • Coinbase (NASDAQ: COIN) fell 8.65% to $174.89, erasing the prior day's 9% rally on pre-vote optimism
  • ~$289M in crypto positions were liquidated around the vote itself (91% long bets); the 24-hour total reached ~$772M across 120,000+ traders
  • Crypto-linked equities fell more than 10% in some cases, outpacing the token-market decline
Analyst read

Not seen as a structural blow

  • Analysts (per The Block) called the defeat "nothing truly structural" — crypto is expected to keep trading on rates and the broader macro backdrop, not U.S. legislative timing
  • The SEC and CFTC retain authority to keep shaping the market through rules and enforcement even without the Act
  • A Fed rate decision the following day (Sept 16) is seen as a bigger near-term price driver than the bill's fate
Prediction-market odds of enactment in 2026 collapsed from roughly 82% in February to single digits by the morning of the vote (Polymarket ~5–17% across various markets), and effectively to near zero once cloture failed.

What this means for India

India has no CLARITY Act of its own — but it does have one of the world's largest bases of retail crypto users, so U.S. rules still reach Indian portfolios indirectly.

No direct legal effect

The Act changes U.S. law only. It does not alter India's own crypto framework — the 30% flat tax on gains, 1% TDS on transactions, and the absence of a dedicated crypto regulator all stay exactly as they are.

Indirect market transmission — now confirmed

India's exchanges largely mirror global prices, and this played out in real time: as Bitcoin slid roughly 3–4% and crypto-linked stocks fell double digits on the failed cloture vote, Indian holders felt the same move on their own platforms — with no change to Indian rules at all.

What Indian traders can buy shifts

Tokens the SEC/CFTC jointly reclassify as commodities (Bitcoin, Ether, Solana and XRP were named in a March 2026 interpretive release) become easier for global market makers to list and support. If a token instead risks being pulled into "security" status, liquidity and listings can thin out — affecting availability on platforms Indian users access.

A template for regulators to study

SEBI and the RBI have historically watched major-market frameworks when shaping their own approach. A finished U.S. statute — splitting oversight between a markets regulator and a commodities regulator — gives Indian policymakers a concrete model to evaluate, even without any obligation to follow it.

Bottom line: nothing about Indian crypto taxation, reporting, or legality changes today. The main channel is price and liquidity — Indian investors are exposed to the same volatility the CLARITY Act creates in the U.S., simply by holding globally-traded assets.
